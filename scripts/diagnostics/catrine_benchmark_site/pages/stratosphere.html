Stratosphere and upper troposphere

The top 10 % of the air mass (above ≈ 100 hPa) and the 60–90 % band (≈ 400–100 hPa). Here the long-lived tracers (CO₂, SF₆) carry the memory of the initial state for years, so offsets and slow drifts matter more than month-to-month noise.

Why ERA5 falls further below GCHP above 100 hPa

  • Where: a tropical deficit between 70 and 30 hPa that grows through 2022 (ERA5 − MERRA-2, 20° S–20° N above 100 hPa: −0.27 ppm CO2 in March, −0.55 in August, −0.75 in November 2022). At 100 hPa ERA5 has slightly more fossil CO2 than MERRA-2: the air reaches the tropopause but rises more slowly above it (latitude–pressure sections).
  • Meteorology: ERA5's own tropical upwelling is weaker. The net upward mass flux across 70 hPa between 20° S and 20° N is 0.65–0.72 × MERRA-2's (≈ 0.6 × in June–November 2022), computed from the raw reanalyses; the transport binaries reproduce raw ERA5 within about 1 %. Ploeger et al. (2021) also find a markedly slower Brewer–Dobson circulation in ERA5 than in ERA-Interim (ACP 21, 8393).
  • Vertical grid: L117 layers in the tropical lower stratosphere are ≈ 0.39 km thick, against ≈ 1.15 km for L66 and L72. Thicker layers carry a tracer front upward faster through vertical numerical diffusion: an earlier ERA5 L66 run with the same upwelling matched GCHP's tropical fossil CO2 at 70 hPa (its face-flux method also differs; the two effects are not separated). GCHP (MERRA-2, L72) includes that diffusion; it is a reference, not the truth.
  • Not a v0.5 change: v0.5 and pre-v0.5 ERA5 L117 mass fluxes agree at every interface from 6 to 230 hPa (RMS ratio 0.9997–1.0000), and the MERRA-2 run shows no such drift through the same code. The signoff therefore tests ERA5's drift criterion on construction invariance and meteorological fidelity, and reports the ERA5 − GCHP trend as attributed transport uncertainty (tracker OF-109).

Why AtmosTransport starts below GCHP above 100 hPa

GCHP's CATRINE initial state places each value of the protocol's initial field at the top interface of its layer instead of at the layer middle: the whole profile is shifted up by half a layer. Reconstructing GCHP's initial field this way matches it to ≤ 0.05 ppm CO₂ (≤ 0.003 ppt SF₆), against 0.62 ppm (0.07 ppt) for mid-layer placement. Because mixing ratios fall with height in the stratosphere, the shift gives GCHP ≈ 0.35 ppm more CO₂ and ≈ 0.046 ppt more SF₆ above 100 hPa than a model that starts from the protocol's initial state, as the v0.5 runs do. The offset relaxes on the stratospheric flushing time, so it is still visible after two years.

This is why the v0.5 signoff judges the stratospheric biases against the pre-v0.5 runs of the same initial state (criteria C1–C4) and judges drift by the trend of the monthly bias (C8), never against zero. A MERRA-2 run started from GCHP's own initial state separates the offset from transport (below).

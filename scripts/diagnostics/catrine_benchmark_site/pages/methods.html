Methods and known differences

What was run, how the comparison is computed, and the differences between the models that are known and explained. GCHP is the reference of this benchmark, not the truth.

The runs

runmeteorology and gridtransport and physicsinitial statestatus

All runs use the CATRINE protocol inputs and write 3-hourly instantaneous fields from 2021-12-01 to 2023-12-31. The AtmosTransport runs use Float32 arithmetic with compensated (Float64) mass totals, PPM horizontal advection and the FV3 kord-8 vertical remap, and run month by month (each month restarts from the previous month's last snapshot; the restarts change the monthly statistics by about 10⁻⁵ relative).

Tracers

  • CO₂: CATRINE initial state and the CAMS (LMDZ) posterior 3-hourly surface fluxes, which include fossil emissions (in AtmosTransport's output this tracer is named co2_natural).
  • Fossil CO₂: GridFED emissions, starting from zero. The protocol's fossil tracer starts on 2022-01-01, but GCHP's starts on 2021-12-01; AtmosTransport carries both, and this site compares the one emitted from 2021-12-01 so the two models see the same emissions.
  • SF₆: protocol initial state and EDGAR emissions scaled to the NOAA growth rate.
  • Rn-222: protocol soil emissions, starting from zero, radioactive decay with the protocol half-life of 3.8235 days.

Mole fractions are dry-air mole fractions in every model. Units: CO₂ and fossil CO₂ in ppm (µmol/mol), SF₆ in ppt (pmol/mol), Rn-222 in 10⁻²¹ mol/mol.

How the comparison is computed

  • One grid. All models write the same GMAO C90 cubed sphere (6 × 90 × 90 columns, about 100 km); the cell corners are checked to agree. Statistics are computed on that grid, never on interpolated maps.
  • Bands of air mass, not levels. Each column is split by the fraction of its dry-air mass counted from the surface: 0–10 % (surface to ≈ 910 hPa), 10–60 % (≈ 910–400 hPa), 60–90 % (≈ 400–100 hPa) and 90–100 % (above ≈ 100 hPa), plus the whole column. Band means are mass weighted, so they do not depend on the models' different level sets (72 and 117 levels).
  • Statistics. Area-weighted over the globe: bias = mean(run − GCHP); RMSE = root of the mean squared difference; r and R² = spatial correlation (coefficient of determination); slope = least-squares slope of run on GCHP. They are computed for every 3-hourly snapshot (daily curves) and for monthly-mean fields (monthly curves, tables).
  • Overview table. The monthly-mean-field statistics averaged over months: bias as a mean, RMSE as the root of the mean square, r as a mean. The months used are those compared for every run on the site, without December 2021, the spin-up month.
  • Period mean (maps and sections): the time-weighted mean of the monthly-mean fields over the common months.
  • Zonal means on fixed dry-pressure levels (900–10 hPa) in 4° latitude bins: each model's profile is interpolated in log pressure on its own grid first.
  • Maps show the C90 fields averaged onto 2° cells; hover values are those 2° means.
  • Mass balance: burden change minus the model's own emissions (and decay), from each model's own diagnostics.

The v0.5 signoff

The signoff asks whether AtmosTransport v0.5 transports these tracers at least as well as the pre-v0.5 runs did: each criterion compares the v0.5 run with the pre-v0.5 run of the same meteorology and initial state, with tolerances set from measured run-to-run spread (for example, switching between the GCHP-matched advection options). Absolute differences to GCHP that pass are reported as transport uncertainty, not judged. The verdict is pending until all 25 months of both signoff runs (ERA5 and MERRA-2) are compared, fail if any blocking criterion fails on any month, pass otherwise. Two raw ratios are reported but not counted because their changes were attributed with evidence to corrections in v0.5 or in the initial-state handling (C5c, C6c).

Known differences

On the GCHP side (deviations from the CATRINE protocol, found in GCHP's 3-hourly diagnostics)

  • Initial state shifted by half a layer: each protocol value sits at its layer's top interface. Above 100 hPa this gives GCHP ≈ 0.35 ppm more CO₂ and ≈ 0.046 ppt more SF₆ than the protocol initial state (see Stratosphere).
  • Fossil CO₂ emitted from 2021-12-01 instead of 2022-01-01 (compared here with a tracer emitted from the same date).
  • SF₆ emissions 0.48 % high: the conversion of the protocol's ppt growth to kg used ≈ 5.139×10¹⁸ kg of air while GCHP's dry air is 5.114×10¹⁸ kg (≈ +0.0019 ppt/yr faster growth); December 2021 uses a different emission map.
  • Rn-222 decays 0.17–0.23 % slower than the protocol half-life (3.83 d instead of 3.8235 d).
  • ColumnMass_* diagnostics are mislabelled (mole fraction × air mass, no molar-mass factor) and 1.3–2.5 % low; GCHP's tracer-flux diagnostics are not budget-consistent. Neither is used here.
  • Emissions are otherwise identical per 3-hour step (fossil, Rn-222; natural CO₂ to 0.06 %), and the MERRA-2 boundary-layer height and dry surface pressure match AtmosTransport's MERRA-2 run (PBLH to 0.006 m RMS; dry air mass to 1.3×10⁻⁵).

On the AtmosTransport side

  • v0.5 corrections that change the numbers compared with earlier AtmosTransport benchmark pages: the dry-air mass now matches GCHP's (98,325 Pa global dry surface pressure; earlier runs had 0.42 % more air, hence lower mole fractions for the same emitted mass), Rn-222 decay is split symmetrically over each meteorological window (removes a −0.38 % bias), the initial state is remapped conservatively onto the model's own surface pressure (earlier runs misplaced it near the surface by up to 7 ppm locally), and the MERRA-2 non-local boundary-layer mixing no longer falls back to surface injection on roundoff (OF-108; the MERRA-2 run shown is the rerun with the fix). Earlier pages are therefore not directly comparable with this one.
  • ERA5 stratospheric drift (OF-109, attributed): above 100 hPa the ERA5 run falls increasingly below GCHP, from a tropical deficit at 70–30 hPa that grows through 2022. ERA5's own tropical upwelling is weaker (net upward mass flux across 70 hPa, 20° S–20° N: 0.65–0.72 × MERRA-2's, ≈ 0.6 × in June–November 2022), the transport binaries reproduce raw ERA5 within about 1 %, and the finer L117 layers (≈ 0.39 km against 1.15 km for L66/L72) carry less vertical numerical diffusion than GCHP's L72 grid. Ploeger et al. (2021, ACP 21, 8393) report a slower Brewer–Dobson circulation in ERA5 than in ERA-Interim. GCHP (MERRA-2, L72) is a reference here, not the truth; the signoff reports the ERA5 − GCHP trend instead of counting it (Stratosphere).
  • Open finding OF-104: AtmosTransport keeps slightly more Rn-222 in the lowest 10 % of the air mass than GCHP (the cause is in the mixing operators; emissions, boundary-layer height, dry pressure and decay are ruled out).
  • Open finding OF-105: in the identical-start MERRA-2 comparison, v0.5 moves SF₆ out of the northern high latitudes (60–90° N) slightly faster than the pre-v0.5 code (−0.001 ppt by February 2022); a transport difference, being attributed.
  • Open finding OF-106: both models use the 1° LMDZ CO₂ flux product and GridFED v2024.0, while the protocol names the 0.1° CAMS product and GridFED v2024.1; the binding versions are being confirmed with the protocol owners.

Rebuilding this site

The site is static (no server code) and is regenerated from the signoff comparison products with one command:

python3 scripts/diagnostics/catrine_benchmark_v05_site.py --out <folder>

Only finished comparison seasons are read, so a rebuild while the runs continue shows the months compared so far; the status line at the top gives the last compared month of each run and the time of the build. Charts are drawn with D3.js 7.9.0 (ISC licence), served from this site; all data files are in data/ (JSON wrapped in JavaScript so the pages also open from disk).

GriddedIO API ​

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO owns the conventions of native gridded input files (surface-flux inventories, land fractions, initial-condition fields). Each rule is a type the caller names, and the methods dispatch on it:

  • Coordinates. find_coordinate finds a coordinate by variable name only (never by units or long_name, which real files mislabel). latlon_orientation, orient and orient_coordinates put a field into ascending latitude and longitudes starting at the first value ≥ 0.

  • Fill values. read_raw with MaskedAsZero, MaskedAsNaN or FillValueOnly, straight into the caller's element type.

  • Source meshes. source_latlon_mesh derives cell faces from a file's centres by a face rule. SnappedFaces (the default): the snapping rule for regular global axes, faces at the centres ± half a cell for longitudes centred on 0° or ±180°, midpoint faces with pole and truncation rules for pole-centred, truncated and Gaussian latitudes. UniformSpanFaces: the uniform mesh the runtime surface-flux and initial-condition loaders used before they moved to SnappedFaces; the runtime no longer uses it. Bounds variables are never used.

  • Time axes. decode_time classifies an axis by its raw units before any CFTime call and decodes it with CFDecoder, MonthsSince, DecimalYears or AuxiliaryTimeVariable; MonthKey, TimeInterval and seconds_since carry the result; decode_slice_intervals gives the interval each slice of a file stands for (the time coverage of a time-varying surface flux), from known_slice_intervals (CF bounds, months since) or stamp_intervals, the regular step of a series of stamps, which a series of several files applies to the stamps of all its files together. Origins are validated, never normalised. decode_time returns proleptic Gregorian DateTimes, so a 360_day 30 February is an error there; decode_month_keys gives the months of any axis in its own calendar.

  • Staging. stage_input decompresses .nc.gz and single-member .nc.tar.gz inputs into a cache.

  • Flux units. parse_flux_units reads every units string in use into FluxUnits; mass_rate_factor converts to kg of a species per second with the molar masses of Parameters.

  • cs_native flux files (the runtime contract of surface_flux.kind = "cs_native", defined once): layout CS_NATIVE_FLUX_DIMS; the writer open_cs_native_writer, write_slices!, publish! and abandon! (start-stamped intervals with time_bnds, mesh geometry and cell areas, no fill values, atomic publication); the streaming reader cs_native_flux_info and read_cs_native_flux!, the runtime's loader, panel by panel into the caller's arrays; the whole-file reader read_cs_native_flux; the cell-area gate check_cell_area.

  • Lat-lon flux files for kind = "file": write_latlon_flux, static fields only, on meshes whose faces the runtime rebuilds exactly from the written centres with SnappedFaces.

  • Observation ground fields ([output.observations] vertical placement): the per-grid model surface height, write_model_surface_height and read_model_surface_height (grid-checked, into Output.ModelSurfaceHeight), and a lat-lon DEM for point lookups, read_point_elevation → LatLonPointElevation.

The rules were measured on the TRENDY v14, GFED5, FLUXCOM-X, CAMS, GridFED and EDGAR files; src/GriddedIO/README.md lists them with their invariants.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO Module
julia
GriddedIO

The one owner of the conventions of native gridded input files (surface-flux inventories, land fractions, initial-condition fields): how their coordinates are found, which values are fill, how a latitude-longitude field is oriented, which cell faces its coordinates imply, how its time axis is decoded, how a compressed input is staged, and how its flux units are read. It also owns the schema of the surface-flux files the model writes and the runtime reads (cs_native and lat-lon), with their one writer and reader.

Every rule here was measured on the files the model reads (the TRENDY v14 S3 models, GFED5, FLUXCOM-X, CAMS/LMDZ, GridFED, EDGAR); the measurements are in docs/memos/2026-10-10_python_flux_port_p0_facts.md. The rules are typed: the caller names an axis (LongitudeAxis), a fill policy (MaskedAsZero), a time decoder (CFDecoder), and the methods dispatch on it.

Contents

  • Coordinates (coordinates.jl): find_coordinate by variable name only, latlon_orientation / orient / orient_coordinates, and read_raw with the fill policies FillValueOnly, MaskedAsNaN and MaskedAsZero.

  • Source meshes (source_meshes.jl): source_latlon_mesh, the cell faces of a file's latitude-longitude grid, by the face rule SnappedFaces (on Grids.LatLonMesh(λᶠ, φᶠ; radius)) or UniformSpanFaces (the legacy uniform-span reconstruction).

  • Time axes (time_axes.jl): decode_time with the decoders CFDecoder, MonthsSince, DecimalYears and AuxiliaryTimeVariable, chosen from the raw units string before any CFTime call, and decode_month_keys, the months of an axis in its own calendar; MonthKey, TimeInterval, month_keys, interval, seconds_since, decode_month_seconds, check_last_month; the interval each slice stands for, decode_slice_intervals (one file), known_slice_intervals and stamp_intervals (a series of files).

  • Staging (staging.jl): stage_input decompresses .nc.gz and single-member .nc.tar.gz inputs into a cache directory.

  • Flux units (flux_units.jl): parse_flux_units → FluxUnits, mass_rate_factor, kg_per_unit, period_seconds, canonical_units_string.

  • The cs_native flux file (cs_native_flux.jl), the runtime contract: CS_NATIVE_FLUX_DIMS, the writer open_cs_native_writer / write_slices! / publish! / abandon!, the streaming reader cs_native_flux_info / read_cs_native_flux! (the runtime's loader, panel by panel into the caller's arrays), the whole-file reader read_cs_native_flux and the cell-area gate check_cell_area.

  • Lat-lon flux files for kind = "file" (latlon_flux.jl): write_latlon_flux.

  • Static ground fields for observation placement (surface_height.jl): the per-grid model surface height dataset, written by write_model_surface_height and read by read_model_surface_height into Output.ModelSurfaceHeight, and a lat-lon DEM for point lookups, read_point_elevation → LatLonPointElevation (bilinear Output.point_elevation).

Molar masses and the year length come from Parameters (SPECIES_MOLAR_MASS, CARBON_MOLAR_MASS, DAYS_PER_JULIAN_YEAR).

src/AtmosTransport.jl loads this module after Regridding and before Preprocessing; it imports Parameters, Grids, Output (the shared cubed-sphere geometry and provenance blocks) and AtomicFiles. See src/GriddedIO/README.md for the file map and invariants.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.AuxiliaryTimeVariable Type
julia
AuxiliaryTimeVariable(variable, origin::Date, unit::Dates.Period, stamp::AbstractMonthStamp)

Decoder of a monthly time axis that is only an index (no units), whose stamps are in another variable of the file. CARDAMOM: time = 1 … 264; the Time variable (units "d", long name "Monthly time step given in days since 01/01/2003") holds 31, 59, 90, …, 8036, the end of each month, so AuxiliaryTimeVariable("Time", Date(2003, 1, 1), Day(1), MonthEnd()) gives 2003-01 … 2024-12. Stamp k is DateTime(origin) + v_k · unit, rounded to the millisecond, where v_k is value k of variable, which must have as many values as the axis; it decodes to the first instant of the month it belongs to under stamp.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.CFDecoder Type
julia
CFDecoder()

Decoder of <unit> since <origin> axes whose unit has a fixed length (day, hour, minute, second, millisecond; also the abbreviations d, h, hr, min, s, sec), through CFTime, honouring the calendar attribute:

  • standard, gregorian, proleptic_gregorian, julian or no calendar: NCDatasets' own decoding, rounded to the millisecond (the runtime's stamps, bit for bit; a julian axis keeps its instants, so its dates shift by the Julian–Gregorian difference);

  • noleap/365_day, all_leap/366_day, 360_day: the calendar date and time of day of each stamp in its own calendar (values rounded to the millisecond first), carried over to a DateTime. Every noleap/365_day date exists in the proleptic Gregorian calendar; an all_leap/366_day or 360_day date that does not (29 February of a common year, 30 February) is an ArgumentError naming it. decode_month_keys takes the months of such axes from the calendar dates themselves.

A trailing " UTC" on the origin is dropped. Fill values in the axis are an error.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.DecimalYears Type
julia
DecimalYears(stamp::AbstractMonthStamp)

Decoder of monthly axes stored as decimal years without usable units (ELM-FATES, month starts: DecimalYears(MonthStart()); VISIT-UT, mid-month, units years since AD 0-Jan-1st (double): DecimalYears(MidMonth())). With m = 12 v months since year 0, the month index is k̂ = round(m − o) (o = 0 for month starts, ½ for mid-month stamps, 1 for month ends) and the residual |m − o − k̂| must not exceed 10⁻⁶ month, else an ArgumentError. Rounding to the nearest month is required: ELM-FATES has 1296 values 9.1e-13 month below the integer, where a plain floor gives 2592 bad steps. Each stamp decodes to the first instant of its month.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.UniformSpanFaces Type
julia
UniformSpanFaces()

Face rule of source_latlon_mesh that gives the uniform mesh of the former runtime surface-flux and initial-condition loaders (Models.InitialConditionIO), bit for bit. The mesh is uniform: for n centres x, the spacing is the mean spacing (x[n] − x[1]) / (n − 1) and the faces lie half a spacing outside the first and last centres. A latitude extent within 1e-3 of a cell of ±90° becomes ±90°; a longitude span within 1e-3 of a cell of 360° becomes exactly 360°, measured from the west face.

Taking the spacing from the whole span, not the first difference, keeps Float32-stored centres from biasing every cell (GridFED stores its 0.1° centres in Float32; their first difference errs by about 1e-4 relative), and the extent snapping keeps the regridder from missing a sliver of the sphere (GridFED: 1.5e-5° of longitude, 2.1e-6 of the global flux). The faces still follow the centres' round-off: GridFED's west face is 7.45e-10°, where SnappedFaces gives 0. The rule assumes uniform, cell-centred centres; pole-centred rows (the 143 LMDZ latitudes of the CATRINE SF6 initial state, CLM, ELM, iMAPLE), truncated and Gaussian latitudes get uniform faces that do not bound their cells.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.check_cell_area Method
julia
check_cell_area(ds, mesh::CubedSphereMesh; file = path of ds) -> nothing

The cell-area gate of a cs_native file. The runtime multiplies the stored densities by mesh.cell_areas; a file prepared on another cube geometry (an equiangular instead of the GMAO cube, another radius) has other cell areas, and its emitted mass would be silently wrong. When the dataset ds has a cell_area variable, shape (Nc, Nc) or (Nc, Nc, 6), each panel a must satisfy isapprox(a, mesh.cell_areas; rtol = 1e-4): the norm-based relative difference ‖a − A‖ ≤ 10⁻⁴ max(‖a‖, ‖A‖) (Frobenius norm over the panel). Fill values in cell_area fail the gate; a file without cell_area passes.

This is the gate the runtime has applied since the cs_native kind exists; legacy files written with Float32-era geometry (per-cell area differences up to 1.75e-4 on C90 polar panels) pass it.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.check_latlon_cell_area Method
julia
check_latlon_cell_area(area, mesh_area, units; file, rtol = 1e-2, flux = nothing) -> area

The cell-area gate of a lat-lon flux file whose cell_area or area variable sets the inventory's total (area [m²], oriented like the flux). Its units must be square metres ("m2", "m^2", "m**2"; an empty string, for an absent attribute, is accepted), and it must agree with mesh_area, the cell areas of the mesh that the file's coordinates define on the runtime sphere, as isapprox(area, mesh_area; rtol): the norm-based relative difference ‖a − A‖ ≤ rtol max(‖a‖, ‖A‖) (Frobenius norm). The default 1 % passes any Earth-radius convention (6.371 versus 6.378137 × 10⁶ m is 0.22 % in area) and the files' own cell definitions, while areas in km² or a land fraction, which would scale the emitted total, fail. A size mismatch is a DimensionMismatch.

With flux (the flux slice, oriented like area), every cell whose flux is nonzero must have a finite, positive area: the norm test alone passes a few masked (zero) rows, such as polar rows, and their emission would be dropped without a word (per-cell units give a cell without area no flux; per-m² units give it no rate).

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.decode_month_seconds Method
julia
decode_month_seconds(ds, name) -> Vector{Float64}

Length in seconds of the calendar month represented by each slice of time axis name. This is the conversion basis for flux variables whose units are per calendar month.

When the coordinate names a CF bounds variable, each slice is assigned from the midpoint of its bounds. For fixed-length CF units the bounds' actual duration is returned; for months since bounds, one calendar month is required. Without bounds, the coordinate stamp assigns the month. Month lengths use the coordinate's calendar (standard by default), including 360_day, 365_day/noleap, and 366_day/all_leap. Thus neither a slice's ordinal position nor the proleptic-Gregorian month length is guessed.

julia
open_cs_native_writer(path, mesh::CubedSphereMesh, intervals::Vector{TimeInterval},
                      variables; species::Symbol, chunk_time::Integer, deflate = 1,
                      attributes = Dict(), config_text = nothing) -> CSNativeFluxWriter
open_cs_native_writer(f, path, mesh, intervals, variables; kwargs...) -> String

Open a cs_native surface-flux file for mesh with one slice per interval. variables lists the flux series, each a name or name => attributes (for example "GPP_CO2_FLUX" => Dict("long_name" => "gross primary production")). The file is written to a staging file next to path and appears at path only when publish! succeeds; a relative path is taken relative to the working directory at opening. The do-block form calls f(writer), then publish!, and returns the absolute path; if f throws, the file is abandoned.

The file (NetCDF-4):

  • Geometry from Output.define_cs_horizontal_geometry!(ds, mesh): dimensions Xdim, Ydim, Xcorner, Ycorner, nf; cell centres lons/lats, corners, cell_area = mesh.cell_areas (Float64, m²) and the cubed_sphere grid mapping with semi_major_axis = mesh.radius.

  • Time: the intervals must be contiguous (stop of one is start of the next). time holds each interval's start (Float64, "hours since <first day> 00:00:00", calendar = "proleptic_gregorian"), time_bnds (nv, time) its start and stop. Slices are start-stamped, so the runtime's stepwise scheme holds each slice for exactly its interval.

  • Series: one Float32 variable per entry of variables, dimensions CS_NATIVE_FLUX_DIMS, units = canonical_units_string(species) ("kg CO2 m-2 s-1" for :co2), sign_convention = "positive into the atmosphere", grid_mapping = "cubed_sphere", coordinates = "lons lats", and no _FillValue. Chunks are (Nc, Nc, 1, chunk_time) (Julia order; chunk_time is clamped to the number of slices), deflate level deflate with shuffle: chunk_time = 1 for daily files and 24 for hourly files (the P0 read benchmarks, design Section 2.4).

  • Global attributes: Conventions = "CF-1.8", species, the provenance block of Output.define_provenance_attributes! (its creation_date is the publication time), config_text and config_sha1 (SHA-1 of the text) when config_text is given, then attributes. A caller attribute may not replace one the writer sets.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.parse_flux_units Method
julia
parse_flux_units(s; substance = nothing, molar_default = nothing) -> FluxUnits

Parse the flux units string s. Case, whitespace, ./* separators and LaTeX exponent markup (m$^{-2}$) are ignored. The grammar is

julia
<amount>[<substance>]<factors>[, <qualifier>]

  • amount: kg, g, tonnes, mol, mmol, µmol/umol;

  • substance (optional, right after the amount): C (carbon) or a species name such as CO2, (CO2);

  • factors, in any order: an area (m-2, /m2, cell-1) and a time (s-1, /s, d-1, /day, month-1, /month, per month, yr-1, /year); without an area the flux is per cell. A time is required, except for Tonnes, the EDGAR convention for tonnes per cell and year;

  • qualifier: only per unit land area occupied by the PFT (SDGVM ra).

substance names the substance when the string does not (TRENDY files write kg m-2 s-1 for kg of carbon: substance = :carbon; ECCO-Darwin writes mmol m-2 s-1 of CO₂: substance = :co2). :c/:carbon give Carbon, other names a Species. A substance named both in the string and by the keyword must agree. Without either, mass units are of the TargetSpecies; molar units without a substance take molar_default (the runtime loaders pass the tracer's species, so mmol m-2 s-1 is moles of that species) and throw when it is nothing.

Examples: "kg m-2 s-1", "kg m-2 s-1", "kg.m-2.s-1", "kg m$^{-2}$ s$^{-1}$", "kgC m-2 s-1", "gC m-2 d-1", "g C per month" (per cell), "kgCO2/month/m2", "Tonnes", "mmol m-2 s-1" (with substance), and the runtime's "kg/m2/s", "kg(CO2) m^-2 s^-1", "kg/m²/s". Anything else throws an ArgumentError.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.read_point_elevation Method
julia
read_point_elevation(path; variable = "z") -> LatLonPointElevation

Read a lat-lon DEM for point lookups. The file holds 1-D longitude and latitude centre coordinates (found by name, find_coordinate) and a 2-D variable variable on those two coordinate dimensions, with the ground elevation in metres above sea level; units must say metres. The axes are identified by the variable's dimension names: (lon, lat) in Julia order (CF/NetCDF order (lat, lon), the usual layout) or the transpose, which is read transposed back; any other dimensions are an error. AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.source_latlon_mesh Function
julia
source_latlon_mesh(lon, lat, rule = SnappedFaces(); radius) -> LatLonMesh{Float64}
source_latlon_mesh(ds, rule = SnappedFaces(); radius) -> LatLonMesh{Float64}

The latitude-longitude mesh of a gridded input file whose cell centres are lon and lat [degrees], with faces derived by the face rule rule (SnappedFaces, described below, or UniformSpanFaces). The second form reads the centres of the dataset's coordinates (find_coordinate). radius [m] has no default: the source mesh must sit on the sphere of the mesh it is regridded to.

Under SnappedFaces, the mesh is built on the explicit-face constructor LatLonMesh(λᶠ, φᶠ; radius); latitude rows of a descending axis are stored ascending (Grids.latitude_row_order gives the data permutation), and longitude columns keep their input order (a wrapped axis is unwrapped, not rolled).

Faces under SnappedFaces

Regular global axes: the snapping rule. For n stored centres x_k, k = 0 … n-1, with res = 180/n (latitude) or 360/n (longitude) and the origin x₀ = -90 (ascending latitude), +90 (descending latitude), -180 (longitude with a negative centre) or 0 (longitude without one), direction s = ±1:

  1. k̂ = round((x_k − x₀)/(s·res) − ½); require k̂ = k and |(x_k − x₀)/(s·res) − ½ − k̂| ≤ 1e-6 cells plus the storage rounding of the centres, 2 eps(T) max|x| / res cells for storage precision T (Float64 values that are all Float32 numbers, for longitudes also after subtracting 360°, count as Float32: GridFED's Float32 0.1° centres sit up to 3e-5 cells off). Otherwise an ArgumentError: a half-cell offset or a wrong resolution.

  2. The faces are x₀ + s·j·res, j = 0 … n, built from integers (the correctly rounded ratio (x₀ n + s j 180)/n), so the outer faces are exactly ±90 and -180/180 (or 0/360).

A longitude axis may start anywhere on that grid (after a roll or a wrap); a global axis whose centres sit on multiples of res (a centre on 0° or ±180°) gets faces at the centres ± res/2, so its first face is not ±180 or 0 (CLASSIC -180.5°, CLM -0.625°, CLM-FATES and ELM-FATES -1.25°, ELM -180.625°, JSBACH -180.9375°). Any other registration throws.

Other latitude axes (pole-centred, truncated, Gaussian, regional): faces at the midpoints of adjacent centres. An outer face is the pole when the outer centre is closer to the pole than the adjacent spacing (pole-centred rows of CLM, ELM and iMAPLE, whose polar cells are half-width caps; the Gaussian JSBACH rows, whose midpoint faces with polar outer faces reproduce the Python audit); otherwise it is the outer centre minus half the adjacent spacing (the FATES grids, truncated south of their row at -59.684°: southern face -60.6316°). Such meshes have non-uniform latitude faces (Δφ = NaN), so dx, dy and other Δφ-based metrics throw.

Regional longitude axes (spanning less than 360°): uniform faces from the first centre minus to the last centre plus half the mean spacing.

Faces are never taken from lat_bnds/lon_bnds.

AtmosTransport.GriddedIO.stamp_intervals Method
julia
stamp_intervals(stamps; source = "the time stamps") -> Vector{TimeInterval}

The interval each of the strictly increasing interval-start stamps (the bare stamps of a whole series, merged over its files) stands for. A series has a regular step, and each slice holds for that step:

  • Month starts (every stamp the first instant of a month): a slice holds until the next stamp when that is one month later, or one year later from a 1 January to the next 1 January (an annual series, also after a monthly part); the last stamp holds for one calendar month, or one year when the last spacing is such a year. A slice before any other spacing (Jan 1 then Mar 1) holds for its own month only, so the months in between are a gap.

  • Other stamps: the step is the smallest spacing; a slice holds until the next stamp when the spacing is that step (within STAMP_SPACING_TOLERANCE), else for one step (a gap follows), and the last stamp holds for one step.

A gap is not an error here: the coverage check of the surface-flux loaders refuses a run only when the run crosses it. These are ArgumentErrors, since the period of a slice cannot be inferred:

  • a single stamp: one stamp gives no spacing, and a 1 January 00Z stamp may stand for a day, a month or a year;

  • stamps about one month (28 to 31 days) or one year (365 to 366 days) apart that are not all month starts, e.g. mid-month stamps (Jan 15, Feb 15, …): whether each slice holds from its stamp or is centred on it is unknown;

  • stamps that do not increase strictly (a stamp in two files).

The remedy is CF bounds on the time coordinate, or interval-start stamps. source names the stamps in these messages.
